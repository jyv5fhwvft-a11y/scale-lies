1 Title slide ~15 sec ▾
Open Slide 1
"Good morning. Let me ask you something first — when was the last time you stepped on a scale and felt good about the number?"
Pause. Let the room chuckle or sigh.
"For most of us, that answer is 'not recently.' And I'm here to tell you tonight that the number you're worried about — the one on the scale — is telling you the wrong story."
2 Three numbers ~90 sec ▾
Open Slide 2
"Before I show you any bodies, hold three numbers in your head."
Point at 35%. Pause.
"Thirty-five percent. That's how much of the weight lost on drugs like Ozempic can be muscle — not fat."
Point at 40%. Pause.
"Forty percent. That's how much visceral fat rises after menopause. Even when the scale doesn't move."
Point at 5×. Pause.
"And five times. That's the difference in muscle loss between women on Ozempic who lift weights — and women who don't."
"BMI measures mass. Body composition measures risk. GLP-1 drugs change the math — but only if you protect what matters."
3 Section 01 intro ~45 sec ▾
Open Slide 3
"Three women. Three BMIs. Woman A is BMI 21. Woman B is BMI 24. Woman C is BMI 36."
"If you had to pick the healthiest one — who would you pick? Shout it out."
Wait for the room. Most will say "Woman A." Give them 5 seconds.
"Most of you said Woman A. Now — let's see who's actually at highest risk."

12 Takeaways + close ~90 sec ▾
Open Slide 12
"Four sentences. Take them with you."
"One — BMI measures mass, not composition."
"Two — Where fat sits matters more than how much."
"Three — Menopause redistributes fat to the abdomen and accelerates muscle loss."
"Four — On GLP-1 therapy, protein and resistance training shifts the loss. Muscle share drops from thirty-five percent to eight percent. That lever is yours to pull."

"One sentence to take home: the injection lowers the weight. What you lose is your choice. Thank you — I'll take questions."
