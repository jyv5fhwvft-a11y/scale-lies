0 Opening — the thesis in three numbers ~90 sec ▾
"BMI measures mass. Body composition measures risk. GLP-1 drugs change the math — but only if you protect what matters. Here are three numbers to hold on to for the next 15 minutes."
Point at each stat window as you say it. Pause after each number. Let it land.
If the 5× stat gets challenged: "It's direction, not precision. The comparison is trained vs. untrained women on GLP-1 — the spread is large and consistent."
01 BMI Misconception — Sim 1 reveal ~2 min ▾
"Three women, three BMIs. Before I reveal — which one is healthiest? Shout it out."
Wait for guesses. Most pick Woman A. Then click Reveal Body Composition. Pause 3 seconds. Let them see the color shift.
"BMI 21 — the 'healthy' number — has the highest visceral fat and the lowest muscle. BMI 24 — the 'overweight' number — has the strongest muscle. The scale told the wrong story."
Close modal. Scroll to Section 02 without explaining the mechanism yet — save it for 03.
If you're running long: skip Woman C entirely. Just A vs. B is enough contrast.
02 Ozempic Reality Check — the red→green flip ~2 min ▾
"Now here's what the injection does. A woman starts at 82 kilograms. Watch what happens to her muscle when she loses 20% — with and without protection."
Leave the toggle OFF. Drag slider slowly from 0% to 20%. Do not narrate during the drag — let the muscle bar drop in silence.
"Twenty percent lost. A third of it came from muscle. For a postmenopausal woman, that's frailty. And the scale — the scale looks great."
Now click the toggle ON. Same 20% — don't touch the slider.
"Same drug. Same 20%. Add protein and resistance training — and the muscle stays. The injection lowers the weight. What you lose is your choice. That lever is yours to pull."
The flip is the punchline. Don't fill the silence. Let the audience watch the bar move and the color change. Say nothing for 3 full seconds. That's the moment they'll remember.
03 Menopause slider — why this hits you hardest ~90 sec ▾
"So why does this matter more for you than for a 30-year-old? Watch the BMI badge on the left. Don't take your eyes off it."
Drag the age slider slowly from 35 → 60. Pause at each stop. Let them see the message change.
"Same weight. Same BMI. Her belly fat has more than quadrupled. Her muscle has dropped by nearly half. That's what estrogen loss does. And the scale never moved."
Scroll to the three timeline cards and the two callout boxes. Read the timeline left-to-right quickly (one sentence each). Land on the "compounding risk" callout.
"By the time a woman starts GLP-1 therapy at 55, she's already lost muscle from menopause. A drug that takes another third as muscle compounds a problem that was already there."
If the slider feels slow: drag in 3 stops (35 → 45 → 52 → 60) rather than continuously. Cleaner visually.
04 Quiz + takeaways — active recall, then close ~2 min ▾
"Before I close — quick check. Which of these three women should be most worried about her metabolic health?"
Let the audience answer aloud. Don't reveal yet. Wait for them to shout "A" or "B" or "C" or "all."
"Not quite. Woman A — BMI 21 — is actually the highest risk. Low muscle, high visceral fat. The scale told the wrong story. Most of you picked B or C — and that's exactly the mistake this talk is about."
"Woman A. BMI 21. The 'healthy' number. And now you know why."
Pause. Scroll to the four takeaways. Read them slowly — bullet by bullet — while the audience reads along.
"One sentence to take home: the injection lowers the weight. What you lose is your choice. That lever is yours to pull. Thank you — I'll take questions."
If someone asks about the 35%→8% figure: "That's the muscle share of weight lost — 35% without resistance training, 8% with. It's the lever, not a guarantee."
