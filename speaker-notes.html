1 Title slide — "The Weight You Lose vs. The Body You Keep" ~15 sec ▾
Open Slide 1
"Good [morning/afternoon]. Let me ask you something first — when was the last time you stepped on a scale and felt good about the number?"
Pause. Let the room chuckle or sigh. Then continue.
"For most of us, that answer is 'not recently.' And I'm here to tell you tonight that the number you're worried about — the one on the scale, the one your doctor writes down, the one on the BMI chart — is telling you the wrong story."
2 Three numbers ~90 sec ▾
Open Slide 2
"Before I show you any bodies, I want you to hold three numbers in your head."
Point at the 35% stat. Pause.
"Thirty-five percent. That's how much of the weight lost on drugs like Ozempic can be muscle — not fat. Muscle you need for your bones, your balance, your blood sugar."
Point at the 40% stat. Pause.
"Forty percent. That's how much visceral fat — the dangerous fat around your organs — rises after menopause. Even when the scale doesn't move."
Point at the 5× stat. Pause.
"And five times. That's the difference in muscle loss between women on Ozempic who lift weights — and women who don't. Five times."
"BMI measures mass. Body composition measures risk. GLP-1 drugs change the math — but only if you protect what matters. Let me show you what I mean."
If the 5× gets challenged: "It's direction, not precision. The comparison is trained vs. untrained women on GLP-1. The gap is large and consistent."
3 Section 01 intro — three women, three BMIs ~45 sec ▾
Open Slide 3
"Three women. Three BMIs. Woman A is BMI 21 — the 'normal weight' number. Woman B is BMI 24 — upper normal. Woman C is BMI 36 — obese."
"If you had to pick the healthiest one, based on that number alone — who would you pick? Shout it out."
Wait for the room. Most will say "Woman A." Some will hedge. Give them 5 seconds.
"Most of you said Woman A. Now — let's see who's actually at highest risk."

Open Slide 7 Open Dashboard · Section 02

11 Sim 4 — quiz, then "try again" ~90 sec ▾
Open Slide 11 Open Dashboard · Section 04
"Quick check. Which of these three women should be most worried about her metabolic health? Woman A, B, C, or all equally?"
Wait for the room's answer. Deliberately click the WRONG answer most people give — Woman B.
"Not quite. Woman A — BMI 21 — is actually the highest risk. Low muscle, high visceral fat. The scale told the wrong story. Most of you picked B or C — and that's exactly the mistake this talk is about."
Tap "Try again". Buttons reset. Now click Woman A (correct).
"Woman A. BMI 21. The 'healthy' number. And now you know why."
12 Takeaways + close ~90 sec ▾
Open Slide 12
"Four sentences. Take them with you."
Read each takeaway slowly. Pause between them. Let the audience read along.
"One — BMI measures mass, not composition. A normal BMI can hide visceral fat and low muscle — the highest-risk profile for postmenopausal women."
"Two — Where fat sits matters more than how much. Visceral fat is metabolically active and inflammatory. Subcutaneous fat is comparatively benign."
"Three — Menopause redistributes fat to the abdomen and accelerates muscle loss. Risk rises even when the scale doesn't move."
"Four — On GLP-1 therapy, protein and resistance training shifts the loss. Muscle share of weight lost drops from thirty-five percent to eight percent. That lever is yours to pull."
Pause. Let the room settle.
"One sentence to take home: the injection lowers the weight. What you lose is your choice. That lever is yours to pull. Thank you — I'll take questions."
If someone asks about 35%→8%: "That's the muscle share of weight lost — 35% without resistance training, 8% with. It's the lever, not a guarantee."
