Presenter Stage Script

The Weight You Lose
vs. The Body You Keep

Ozempic, menopause, and the weight loss myth · 15 Minutes

0:00 - 1:30
The Opening
▾
🎬 ACTION: Stand still. Look at the audience. Do not touch the screen yet.
"Good [morning/afternoon]. Before I start, I want to ask you something. When was the last time you stepped on a scale and felt truly good about the number?"
[ PAUSE — Let the room react ]
"For most of us, that answer is 'not recently.' And I'm here to tell you tonight that the number you're worried about—the one on the scale, the one on the BMI chart—is telling you the wrong story."
👉 ACTION: Tap → to advance to Slide 2 (Three Numbers).
"Before we look at any bodies, I want you to hold three numbers in your head. Thirty-five percent. That’s how much of the weight lost on drugs like Ozempic can be pure muscle—not fat. Forty percent. That’s how much visceral fat rises after menopause, even when the scale barely moves. And five times. That’s the difference in muscle loss between women on Ozempic who lift weights, and women who don't."
"BMI measures mass. But body composition measures risk."
1:30 - 4:00
The BMI Misconception
▾
👉 ACTION: Advance to Slide 3. Ask the audience to shout out their guess. Then tap Open Dashboard · Section 01.
"Let’s look at three women. Woman A is BMI 21. Woman B is BMI 24. Woman C is BMI 36. If you had to pick the healthiest one based on those numbers, who would you pick? Shout it out."
[ PAUSE — Wait for audience to say "Woman A" ]
"Most of you said Woman A. Let's look inside."
👆 ACTION: On the dashboard, tap Reveal Body Composition. The X-ray appears.
"Look at Woman A. She has the lowest muscle mass and the highest visceral fat of all three. She is what we call 'skinny fat.' Look at Woman B. She has a higher BMI, but she has strong muscle and very little visceral fat. Woman B is actually the healthiest of the three. The scale told you the wrong story."
👆 ACTION: Tap ← Back to slide 4 to return to the presentation. Then tap → to advance.
4:00 - 7:30
The Ozempic Reality Check
▾
👉 ACTION: Advance to Slide 7. Tap Open Dashboard · Section 02. This is the climax. Do not talk while the bar moves.
"So we agree the scale lies. Now, what about the injection? If BMI can't tell us who is healthy, what can Ozempic tell us? Let me show you what 20% weight loss actually takes off."
👆 ACTION: On the dashboard, leave the toggle OFF. Slowly drag the slider to 20%. The muscle bar drops. The verdict turns RED.
"Twenty percent lost. But look at the red warning. A third of that weight loss came from muscle. For a postmenopausal woman, that is the fast track to frailty. And the scale? The scale looks great."
👆 ACTION: Click the Toggle ON. Watch the muscle bar jump back up and the verdict turn GREEN. Pause for 3 full seconds. Let the room see the green.
"Same drug. Same 20% weight loss. But add protein and resistance training, and the muscle stays."
"The injection lowers the weight. But what you lose is your choice. That lever is yours to pull."
👆 ACTION: Tap ← Back to slide 7. Then tap → to advance.
7:30 - 10:00
Menopause Rewrites the Map
▾
👉 ACTION: Advance to Slide 9. Tap Open Dashboard · Section 03. Point directly at the BMI badge on the screen.
"So why does this matter more for you than for a 30-year-old? Menopause rewrites the map. Watch the BMI badge on the left. Don't take your eyes off it."
👆 ACTION: Drag the age slider from 35 to 60. The body changes. The belly grows red.
"Same weight. Same BMI of 24. But her belly fat has more than quadrupled, and her muscle has dropped by nearly half. That is what estrogen loss does. And the scale never moved."
"By the time a woman starts GLP-1 therapy at 55, she has already lost muscle from menopause. A drug that takes another third as muscle compounds a problem that was already there."
👆 ACTION: Tap ← Back to slide 9. Then tap → to advance.
10:00 - 13:00
The Quiz
▾
👉 ACTION: Advance to Slide 11. Tap Open Dashboard · Section 04. Ask the question and let the audience answer.
"Before I close, let's do a quick check. Which of these three women should be most worried about her metabolic health?"
👆 ACTION: Let them shout. Deliberately click Woman B or Woman C (the wrong answer). The button turns RED, and Woman A turns GREEN.
"Not quite. Woman A is green because she is the correct answer to the risk question. She has the highest visceral fat. The scale told you she was safe, but her body says otherwise. That is the mistake this entire talk is about."
👆 ACTION: Tap Try again, then click Woman A. The green text confirms the correct answer.
👆 ACTION: Tap ← Back to slide 11. Then tap → to advance.
13:00 - 15:00
Takeaways & Close
▾
👉 ACTION: Advance to Slide 12. Step away from the iPad. Look directly at the audience.
"Let me leave you with four sentences you can defend at dinner tonight."
"One: BMI measures mass, not composition. A normal BMI can hide visceral fat and low muscle."
"Two: Where fat sits matters more than how much. Visceral fat is inflammatory; subcutaneous fat is benign."
"Three: Menopause redistributes fat to the abdomen and accelerates muscle loss, even when the scale doesn't move."
"Four: On GLP-1 therapy, protein and resistance training shifts the loss. Muscle share drops from 35% down to 8%. That lever is yours to pull."
[ PAUSE — Let the room settle ]
"One sentence to take home: The injection lowers the weight. What you lose is your choice. Thank you—I'll take questions."
