Indications & Boundaries

The Anti-Aging Marketplace: Where Indications Can Blur

GLP-1 medications are highly effective obesity therapies, but demand increasingly extends beyond evidence-based indications.

Treatment

Obesity/adiposity-related disease.

Prevention

Prevention of complications.

Cosmetic

Cosmetic weight reduction.

Anchor prescribing to: indication, expected health benefit, safety, affordability, and long-term feasibility.
Do not equate a lower number on the scale with improved health.

Patient Selection

Who Is an Appropriate Candidate?

Consider anti-obesity pharmacotherapy with health-behavior treatment for adults with:

BMI ≥ 30 kg/m²

Regardless of comorbidities.

BMI ≥ 27 kg/m²

With weight-related complications.

Look Beyond BMI

  • Central adiposity
  • Metabolic disease
  • Sleep apnea
  • Cardiovascular disease
  • Functional limitation
  • Weight trajectory

Confirm that the chosen product and indication match the patient—not a compounded or off-label "wellness" substitute.

Benefits Beyond the Scale

Semaglutide 2.4 mg

FDA indication to reduce cardiovascular death, MI, and stroke in adults with established CVD and overweight/obesity.

Tirzepatide

FDA-approved for moderate-to-severe obstructive sleep apnea in adults with obesity.

Prediabetes

3 years of tirzepatide produced sustained weight reduction and substantially less progression to T2DM vs placebo.

Match the drug to the patient's complication profile whenever possible.

Menopause & Body Composition

Menopause Changes the Conversation

  • Aging is a major driver of midlife weight gain through declining energy expenditure and loss of lean mass.
  • Menopause independently favors redistribution toward abdominal/visceral fat as estrogen declines—even when total weight changes modestly.
  • Sleep disturbance, vasomotor symptoms, mood changes, reduced activity, and medications can amplify weight and metabolic changes.

Assess:

  • Waist circumference
  • Strength / function
  • Sleep
  • Cardiometabolic risk
  • Diet quality
  • Symptoms

— NOT weight alone —

Preserving Lean Mass

The "Thin" Trap: Preserve Lean Mass and Bone

Weight loss includes both fat mass and lean tissue; midlife adults already face age-related muscle loss.

Before & During GLP-1 Therapy:

  • Prioritize adequate protein intake
  • Progressive resistance exercise
  • Assessment of functional decline

Consider Nutrition Referral When:

  • Intake is markedly reduced
  • GI adverse effects persist
  • Frailty/sarcopenia risk is present

Menopause care still requires independent attention to bone health, cardiovascular risk, sleep, and menopausal symptoms.

Safety Profile

Safety: Discuss Before the First Dose

Common (dose escalation)

  • Nausea, vomiting
  • Diarrhea, constipation
  • Abdominal discomfort/reflux

Important Warnings

  • Pancreatitis
  • Gallbladder disease
  • Dehydration/AKI
  • Hypersensitivity
  • Hypoglycemia (with insulin/secretagogues)

Contraindications

  • Personal/family history of medullary thyroid carcinoma
  • MEN2 (for products with boxed warning)

Review pregnancy plans and peri-procedural/anesthesia considerations; follow current product labeling and specialty guidance.

Chronic Disease Management

Set Expectations: Treatment Is Usually Long-Term

STEP 1 Extension Data

  • After 68 weeks of semaglutide, mean weight loss was 17.3%.
  • One year after withdrawal, participants regained 11.6 percentage points—about two-thirds of the prior loss.
  • Many cardiometabolic improvements moved back toward baseline after discontinuation.

Counsel Before Starting:

  • Is long-term therapy acceptable?
  • Can the patient afford/access it?
  • What is the maintenance plan?
  • What if coverage stops?

Clinical Workflow

Prescribing Checklist

1

Establish diagnosis and indication: BMI + central adiposity + weight-related complications + trajectory.

2

Define a health-centered goal: metabolic risk, mobility, OSA, cardiovascular risk, function—not simply clothing size.

3

Screen for contraindications/precautions: glucose-lowering therapy, pregnancy plans, GI history, and procedure plans.

4

Start low and titrate per label: address nutrition, hydration, protein, and resistance activity from day 1.

5

Reassess tolerability, response, adherence, function/body-composition concerns, and continued clinical value.

Practical Strategy

Navigating Insurance: A 5-Minute Office Strategy

Before Prescribing

  • Verify the pharmacy benefit and exact anti-obesity medication coverage.
  • Use the plan's preferred agent when clinically appropriate.
  • Confirm prior authorization, step therapy, and lifestyle-documentation requirements.

When Submitting

  • Submit diagnosis + anthropometrics + comorbidities + prior attempts + rationale as one coherent packet.

If Denied

  • Identify the exact unmet criterion before appealing—do not simply resubmit.

Continuity & Cost

  • Discuss early: loss of coverage can become a clinical issue because weight regain after discontinuation is common.

Back to the Case

Case Revisited: What Would You Do?

Her BMI of 26.4 kg/m² without a qualifying weight-related comorbidity does not meet the usual labeled BMI threshold for chronic weight-management pharmacotherapy.

Incorrect

Prescribing off-label for cosmetic weight loss in a patient who does not meet the BMI threshold exposes her to side effects without a clear evidence-based benefit. This is exactly the "anti-aging marketplace" trap.

Correct

Address menopause symptoms, sleep, resistance training, nutrition, waist/body-composition trajectory, and cardiometabolic risk. If future clinical criteria for pharmacotherapy are met, revisit treatment using a health-centered goal and long-term plan.

The goal is not to make every patient thinner. It is to make the right patient healthier.

Summary

Take-Home Messages

GLP-1/GIP-based therapies are powerful treatments for obesity and selected obesity-related complications—not generic anti-aging drugs.

Menopause often changes fat distribution and body composition more than the scale suggests; protect muscle, bone, sleep, and metabolic health.

Discuss benefits, adverse effects, long-term treatment expectations, and possible weight regain before initiation.

Excellent documentation is clinical care: diagnosis, complications, prior treatment, rationale, goals, and monitoring should be explicit.
